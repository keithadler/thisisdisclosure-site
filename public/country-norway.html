Country guide

Norway.

The Norwegian Penal Code of 2005 makes it an offense to fail to help a person in obvious danger of losing their life or suffering serious injury, up to the limit of one's ability and without exposing oneself or others to special danger. The state also gives a tax deduction for gifts to voluntary organizations, pays VAT compensation to them, and routes a share of a state-licensed betting stake to local voluntary groups chosen by players.

Is there a legal duty to help a stranger?

Yes

Straffeloven section 287 ("Forsømmelse av hjelpeplikt", Neglect of duty to assist) punishes with a fine or up to 6 months in prison anyone who fails, according to ability, to help a person in obvious danger of losing their life or suffering considerable bodily or health harm, or to avert a fire, flood, explosion or similar accident endangering human life. The text says breach is not punished when the duty could not be met without exposing oneself or others to particular danger or sacrifice; the wording was amended in 2025.

Are people who help protected?

Limited

Straffeloven section 17 (necessity) says an act that would otherwise be punishable is lawful when it is done to save life, health, property or another interest from a danger of harm that cannot be averted in another reasonable way, and the risk of harm is far greater than the risk of harm from the act. No separate Good Samaritan liability statute was located in the texts reviewed.

How the government promotes giving and service

Tax deduction for gifts to voluntary organizationsThe Tax Administration says a money gift of at least NOK 500 to a voluntary organization or religious or belief-based community can be deducted, with a maximum deduction of NOK 25,000 per year for income year 2025. One thing worth knowing

Straffeloven section 288 separately punishes failing to help a person in a helpless state whom one has a duty to care for, so Norwegian law pairs a general duty to help with a specific duty of care.
