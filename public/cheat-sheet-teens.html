Cheat sheet for your teens.

Remember: this site is 18+ only. This sheet is for you to read and hand to your teens, not for them to browse the site. They are not too young for the practice. They may be the best at it, because they haven't learned to look away yet.

  1. Sit with the one who is alone.
    Lunch, the bus, a party. That is the whole move.
    Say"Mind if I sit here?"
  2. Be the "that's enough."
    When a group piles on someone, you don't have to win. or just don't laugh. One person not laughing changes the room.
    Say"Okay, that's enough,"
  3. Defend them in private if public is too hard.
    Message them"That wasn't okay. I'm sorry nobody said it."
  4. Do one invisible kindness.
    No post, no screenshot. Do it, then notice what it does to your chest. That is data.
  5. Give one conversation your whole attention.
    Phone face down. Listen to the end.
    Ask"What's the hardest part of this week?"
  6. Say a specific thank-you.
    To a teacher or a parent: "When you ___, it changed ___."
  7. Tell the truth when lying is free.
    Do it even when no one would ever know.
    Say"Actually, that was me."
  8. Give the credit away.
    Let it belong to the group.
    Say"That was her idea."

The evidence behind this sheet

School trials suggest peer-led efforts and bystander training can modestly reduce conflict and increase helping, and kindness tasks can raise peer acceptance. Well-being gains from kind acts in teens are small, uneven, and sometimes absent.

Where the evidence is thin. Many of these trials are small, short, or done in one country, and several found no overall effect. The sheet's specific moves (sitting with someone, a private message, a specific thank-you) are not tested one by one, and these findings do not mean teens should handle serious harm alone.
If you only do one thing this week
Sit with someone, stand up for someone, thank someone.

If someone is being hurt, tell a trusted adult. You do not have to handle it alone, and not every hardship is a test.

This is practical, not religious. I think what you show with your choices is what counts. Share it freely.
