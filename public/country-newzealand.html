Country guide

New Zealand.

New Zealand's Crimes Act 1961 imposes duties to provide necessaries and take care on people who have charge of others or who undertake dangerous acts. The Charities Act 2005 sets up a register of charitable entities, and Inland Revenue allows individuals a tax credit of one third of eligible donations. Royal honours include the King's Service Order and Medal for voluntary community service and the New Zealand Bravery Awards.

Is there a legal duty to help a stranger?

Limited

The Crimes Act 1961 imposes legal duties on people who have actual care or charge of a vulnerable adult (section 151) or a child under 18 (section 152), and on anyone who undertakes a lawful act dangerous to life (section 155) or an act whose omission may be dangerous to life (section 157). The sections reviewed do not contain a duty on a stranger with no such relationship or undertaking to rescue another person.

Crimes Act 1961 | New Zealand Legislation (Parliamentary Counsel Office, New Zealand)

Are people who help protected?

No general duty found

No statute giving general civil or criminal protection to a good-faith helper was located in this review. Section 155 of the Crimes Act excepts "a case of necessity" from the duty of skill and care for people who undertake treatment, but this is not a Good Samaritan clause. Whether other schemes (for example accident compensation) cover helpers was not checked.

One thing worth knowing

The bravery honours are defined around saving another person's life at personal risk and are open to nomination by any member of the public, so recognition of rescue by strangers is built into the Royal honours system.
