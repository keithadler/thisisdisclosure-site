Cheat sheet for people in charge.

The test asks more of you: more people can see you, and fewer will tell you the truth.

  1. Choose on merit, out loud.
    Put the qualified person in the job, not your friend or relative. Be able to say to the runner-up's face: "I chose them because..."
  2. Own the miss, publicly.
    Give the credit away in the same room.
    Say"That was my call. It was wrong. Here is the fix."
  3. Deliver bad news early.
    Say"You would want to know this today, so I'm telling you today."
  4. Hear the lowest-status person first.
    Say"Before I weigh in, Sam, what are you seeing?"
  5. Share what you know.
    Default to open.
    Ask"Who is hurt by not knowing this?"
  6. Reward the one who disagrees.
    Then change something visible.
    Say"Thank you for telling me I'm wrong."
  7. Pay fairly. Never profit from desperation.
    If the deal only works because the other side has no choice, it's the wrong deal.
  8. Offer the first step toward peace.
    Do it before you are forced to.
    Say"I'll move first if you move second."
  9. Run the stranger test.
    Would you make this decision if the person it hurts could watch you make it?
  10. Know when to step aside.
    If someone else would do it better, make room, and help them start.

The evidence behind this sheet

Research links psychological safety, fair treatment, and humble or servant styles of leading with better team learning, trust, and engagement. Most of it is correlational, and the newer leadership labels overlap heavily with older ones.

Where the evidence is thin. Most workplace studies are surveys taken at one point in time, so they cannot show that a leader's behavior caused better teamwork, and several leadership labels overlap. These sheets do not claim that any single action reliably improves results.
If you only do one thing this week
One hard truth told early. One credit given away. One first step toward peace.

This is practical, not religious. I think what you show with your choices is what counts. Share it freely.
