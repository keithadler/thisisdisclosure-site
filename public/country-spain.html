Spain.

Spain's Criminal Code (Ley Orgánica 10/1995) makes failing to help a person who is helpless and in manifest, grave danger a criminal offense when help can be given without risk to oneself or others. Separately, Ley 45/2015 de Voluntariado sets a legal framework for volunteering through organizations, and Ley 49/2002 gives tax incentives for donations to qualifying nonprofits.

Is there a legal duty to help a stranger?

Yes

Article 195 of the Criminal Code (title: 'De la omisión del deber de socorro') punishes anyone who does not help a person who is helpless and in manifest and grave danger, when this can be done without risk to oneself or third parties, with a fine of three to twelve months. Paragraph 2 applies the same penalty to a person who is unable to help but does not urgently request outside aid, and paragraph 3 sets prison terms where the person who omitted help had caused the victim's situation by accident (6 to 18 months) or through negligence (6 months to 4 years).

Are people who help protected?

Limited

No statute specific to good-faith rescuers was found in the sources reviewed. The Criminal Code's general 'estado de necesidad' provision (art. 20.5) exempts from criminal responsibility a person who, to avoid harm to oneself or another, injures a legal interest or breaches a duty, if the harm caused is not greater than the harm avoided and the necessity was not intentionally provoked by that person. Under Ley 45/2015, civil liability for harm caused by volunteers in organized programs rests with the volunteer organization toward third parties.

How the government promotes giving and service

Ley 45/2015 de Voluntariado: definition and rightsThe law defines volunteering as solidarity-based, freely chosen, unpaid activity of general interest carried out through volunteer organizations (art. 3). One thing worth knowing

Ley 45/2015 art. 3.3 states that isolated or sporadic acts outside volunteer organizations, and acts done for family, friendship or good-neighbor reasons, are not 'voluntariado' under the law, so its protections and incentives are aimed at organized volunteering.

What this does not mean

This guide relies on Spanish-language statute texts read in the original; quoted meanings are summaries, not official translations. Article 195 sets a minimum duty and says nothing about rewarding rescuers, and tax percentages and rules may have changed since the consolidated texts consulted. This is not legal advice.
