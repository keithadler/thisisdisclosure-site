Country guide

France.

French criminal law makes it an offense to deliberately fail to help a person in peril when one could do so without risk to oneself or others. A 2020 law gives anyone who voluntarily helps a person in grave and imminent danger the legal status of 'citoyen sauveteur' (citizen rescuer). The state also supports giving and volunteering through a donation tax reduction and a government-funded civic service program.

Is there a legal duty to help a stranger?

Yes

Article 223-6 of the Code pénal punishes anyone who deliberately fails to give a person in peril the help they could have given 'without risk to themselves or third parties', either by personal action or by summoning help. The same article punishes deliberately failing to prevent, by immediate action and without such risk, a crime or an offense against a person's bodily integrity. Penalties are five years' imprisonment and a 75,000 euro fine, raised to seven years and 100,000 euros when the person in peril is a minor under fifteen.

Article 223-6 - Code pénal (Légifrance (French government))

Are people who help protected?

Yes

Law no. 2020-840 of July 3, 2020 added a paragraph II to Article L721-1 of the Code de la sécurité intérieure: anyone who voluntarily assists a person in an apparent situation of grave and imminent peril is a 'citoyen sauveteur' and has the status of 'collaborateur occasionnel du service public' (occasional collaborator of the public service). The article also provides that a citizen rescuer is exempt from civil liability for harm resulting from the intervention except in cases of gross or intentional fault, and that criminal negligence is assessed in light of the urgency and the information available.

How the government promotes giving and service

One thing worth knowing

France combines a criminal duty to help (Article 223-6) with a statute that gives untrained or unaffiliated bystanders who help the status of occasional collaborator of the public service, which the ministerial answer describes as meant to strengthen their legal security even outside any association.
