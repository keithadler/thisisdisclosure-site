Country guide

Russia.

The Criminal Code of the Russian Federation contains a narrow offense of leaving a helpless person in danger (art. 125). Federal Law No. 135-FZ of 11 August 1995, now titled "On Charitable Activities and Volunteering", covers both charity and volunteering and gives volunteers listed rights, including support such as insurance in cases the law provides. Federal bodies are tasked with state programs and a unified information system for volunteering, and the DOBRO.RF platform is presented as that system.

Is there a legal duty to help a stranger?

Limited

Article 125 of the Criminal Code punishes knowingly leaving without help a person who is in a condition dangerous to life or health and cannot protect themselves because of minor age, old age, illness or helplessness. The text applies only where the offender could have helped and either was obliged to care for the person or put the person in danger, so it is not a general duty to assist any stranger (reading of the Russian text as shown on the source page; no official English translation was used).

Статья 125. Оставление в опасности (УК РФ, ред. от 04.08.2026) (ConsultantPlus (non-commercial online version of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation))

Are people who help protected?

Limited

No statute giving a helper general immunity for good-faith aid to a stranger was found. Article 17.1 of Federal Law No. 135-FZ, as added by Federal Law No. 15-FZ of 5 February 2018, lists rights of volunteers, including support in cases provided by law, and provides for insurance of volunteers' life or health in specified circumstances; this concerns organized volunteering, not spontaneous aid.

Федеральный закон от 05.02.2018 N 15-ФЗ (ред. от 21.12.2021) О внесении изменений в отдельные законодательные акты по вопросам добровольчества (волонтерства) (rulaws.ru (legal text aggregator; amending law text))

One thing worth knowing

Russia folded volunteering into its 1995 charity law in 2018, so one statute now governs both and defines volunteer rights and the roles of authorities.

What this does not mean

This guide describes statutes and programs as published and does not assess how they are applied. Translations from Russian are by this guide; the cited legal texts are from non-governmental aggregators of official texts, so the official publication (pravo.gov.ru) should be checked for current wording. This is a plain-language summary of public sources, not legal advice, and it does not rank countries. No government named here has endorsed this site.
